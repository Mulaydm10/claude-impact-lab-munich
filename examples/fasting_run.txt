  overall: 63   verdict: check_flagged

  The research asks whether intermittent fasting (IF) or time-restricted eating (TRE) delivers metabolic benefits beyond what an equivalent calorie deficit from conventional eating would provide, aiming to inform a corporate wellness program decision. The source set includes two calorie-matched RCTs (s1: Lin et al. 2024 in Annals of Internal Medicine, n=41, 12 weeks; s2/s3: ChronoFast 2025 in Science Translational Medicine, n=31, 2×2 weeks crossover), two systematic reviews that attempt to separate isocaloric from ad libitum designs (s4, s7), two additional meta-analyses (s6, s9), and one older secondary analysis (s5). The two calorie-matched RCTs both found no fasting-specific improvement in insulin sensitivity, glucose, or cardiometabolic markers, but both are small and one (ChronoFast) actually compares early-TRE vs. late-TRE rather than TRE vs. unrestricted eating—a design nuance that matters greatly. The meta-analysis in s6 reports a modest short-term HOMA-IR advantage for fasting strategies, but the excerpt does not clarify whether all included trials were truly calorie-matched, so that finding may partly reflect inadvertent calorie deficits. The systematic review in s7 specifically pools isocaloric trials and concludes evidence of a unique IF benefit for insulin sensitivity is 'very limited' and insufficient for firm conclusions, but its literature search predates the 2023–2025 studies the intent specifically requires. One key source (s8, a JCEM article) returned a 403 error and contributes nothing. Overall, the evidence tilts toward IF benefits being largely attributable to calorie reduction, but the source set does not conclusively settle the question: the calorie-matched RCTs are underpowered, the most relevant meta-analyses either lack isocaloric subgroup separation or predate recent trials, and one pivotal study's design is easily mischaracterized. A corporate decision-maker should treat this evidence base as suggestive but insufficient to justify IF-specific program guidance over general calorie-management strategies, and should flag the need for the larger, longer calorie-matched trials that all reviews call for.
