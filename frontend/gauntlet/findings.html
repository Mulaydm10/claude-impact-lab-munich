What we found
testing our own tool

We ran Glassbox against three research briefs — one rigged, one careful, one a real live agent output — and read the results honestly. Here is where it is wrong.

02 / The finding

The reasoning discriminates. The verdict band does not.

All three runs land in the same bottom band — do_not_rely — even though one of them fabricated its sources and the other two did not.

Read the reasoning inside each run and it clearly tells the cases apart: the rigged case gets torn apart for inventing domains and misquoting a headline; the control case is criticised for citing real, careful, peer-reviewed research that simply doesn't measure the exact population asked about; the live-agent case gets credit for five claims that are faithfully sourced. That distinction is real and it is visible in the text. It just never reaches the score band. A rigged brief and a careful one currently print the same verdict word.

03 / Why

A survey answer becomes an absolute bar

The survey stage asks what standard of evidence you want, then compounds that answer into the intent every later stage is judged against. It doesn't stay a preference — it becomes a pass/fail line.

Told
Peer-reviewed preferred. State limitations plainly.
Produced
An Intent demanding evidence through 2023, with effect sizes for developers specifically — a bar almost no real research clears, including the real arXiv and NBER papers the control case actually cited.
Result
Glassbox currently reads stricter than an expert human. That's the safer direction to be wrong in — it under-trusts rather than over-trusts. It is still wrong, and the fix is calibration at the intent stage, not the scoring.
04 / Two bugs we caught by running it

Both found in the live-agent run

✕ Bug

Hedges get marked UNSUPPORTED

The control case says plainly that its call-centre RCT "should not be transferred without caveat" to software engineers. That sentence is the caution — it needs no citation, it is the researcher being honest. Glassbox scored it unsupported anyway, because no source was found to back a claim that was never a claim.

A hedge is an epistemic caution, not an assertion that needs evidence. The pipeline doesn't yet tell the two apart.

Source: control_run.txt, claim c3 — "provenance=model, sources=—, reasoning: no sources were available or plausibly relevant to check this claim against."
✕ Bug

A source it fetched itself got falsely accused

Source s2 in the live-agent run — a METR update dated February 2026 — was fetched successfully, scored 72/100 credibility with 92 relevance, and categorised primary. Every reviewer pass still flagged it as failing the deal-breaker for unverifiable 2026 publications, treating a source it had itself already retrieved as if it might not exist.

The cause is a recency cap the survey stage invented — mid-2025 — that the run itself crossed. The tool contradicted its own fetch.

Source: live_agent_run.txt, source s2 (metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update) and claim c3 review notes.
05 / What it got right

The second reviewer does its job

"The internal review was far too lenient."

— second reviewer, on the first pass's grading

The two-reviewer disagreement design works. The second reviewer, briefed to distrust the first one's leniency, caught exactly that — the first pass had let claims resting on a truncated, advocacy-run source through as "partial" when almost nothing in the excerpt had actually been read yet.

0 phantom citations the pipeline extracted from the research prose itself — assertions like "several sources report… 40%" that referenced nothing in the six sources it was actually given.
06 / What's next

Three fixes, ranked by how much they'd move the score

  • △ Hedge vs. claimTeach the claim-checker to recognise a stated limitation as caution, not an assertion needing its own citation.
  • △ Calibrate the intent stageStop compounding a user's stated preference into an absolute bar. "Peer-reviewed preferred" should not become "nothing else counts."
  • △ Delayed outcome loopRate the recommendation against reality months later, and feed that back. Designed, not built. Not built
