01 · The thesis

A credible source proves nothing if the research misused it.

Credibility

"Is this evidence any good?"

Score 0–100 per source, with concrete reasons and red flags. This is the question almost every fact-checking tool already answers.

Provenance

"Did the research report it honestly?"

Per claim: does a source actually say this, or was it generalised, invented, or stretched past what the evidence supports? Most tools are blind to this entirely.

03 · The scoring formula

Computed in Python, not by the model

weight_i = max(relevance_to_intent_i, 5) base = sum(score_i * weight_i) / sum(weight_i) penalty = share of sources unreachable or uncategorised overall = clamp(base - penalty * 40, 0, 100)

Relevance-weighting is the point. A weak source that answers your actual question should hurt far more than a weak source that is off-topic — otherwise a pile of irrelevant-but-decent citations can drown out the one bad source that actually matters.

04 · The four claim verdicts

Every load-bearing claim, checked against the actual fetched source

●
SUPPORTED

A source directly states this.

◐
PARTIAL

Related but weaker or narrower than the claim actually made.

○
UNSUPPORTED

Nothing in the sources backs it.

✕
CONTRADICTED

A source says the opposite.

Provenance: model

Claims the model introduced with nothing behind them are marked MODEL provenance rather than passed off as fact. That pairing — invented, and unsupported — is the headline finding, and gets the loudest treatment in the UI.

05 · The two reviewers

A second opinion, deliberately adversarial

Internal reviewer

Runs the adversarial re-check stance against every claim: refuses "merely discusses the topic" as support, and downgrades SUPPORTED to PARTIAL wherever a claim generalises beyond its source's scope.

Second anchor

A genuinely different system prompt — framed as an outside auditor, explicitly told to distrust the first reviewer's leniency. Disagreements between the two are computed deterministically and ranked by severity: supported-vs-contradicted outranks supported-vs-partial.

"The internal review was far too lenient." Real quote, live run — second anchor on the internal reviewer
